1. Persistence is extremely effective for one-hour AQI forecasting.
2. Performance declines consistently as the horizon increases.
3. Current AQI remains useful at six hours but loses much of its value by 24 hours.
4. Persistence has almost no explanatory power at 48 hours.
5. At 72 hours, persistence performs worse than predicting the average AQI.
6. Longer-horizon models must use weather, pollutants, temporal patterns, lags, and rolling features.
7. Any trained model should be compared against these baseline scores.
8. The 72-hour model should aim to achieve:
MAE below 18.82
RMSE below 25.20
R² above -0.181
Ideally, it should also produce a positive and meaningfully higher R².

Ridge Regression outperformed both the persistence and training-mean baselines across every forecast horizon.
Its strongest performance occurred at the one-hour horizon, where it achieved an MAE of 1.35, RMSE of 2.55, and R² of 0.988.

At the 72-hour horizon, Ridge Regression achieved an MAE of 15.25, RMSE of 19.80, and R² of 0.270.
This represented a substantial improvement over persistence, which produced a negative R² at the same horizon.

Although prediction accuracy decreased as the forecast horizon increased, the positive R² at 48 and 72 hours showed
that the engineered features contained useful longer-term forecasting information.
